about

Built for operations, not just reporting

I've spent most of my career on the operational side of a business — inventory, procurement, vendor coordination, sales — before moving into the analysis that explains it. That combination is what I bring to business analyst work: I know what the numbers are describing, not just how to chart them.

I completed an MBA in International Business and Information Technology while working as a Business Operations Executive, building Power BI dashboards and Excel reporting frameworks from real sales, inventory and procurement data. My MBA also gave me a working toolkit of structured analysis — SWOT, PESTEL, ABC analysis, risk analysis, and financial ratios — that I apply to real decisions rather than case studies. I also use AI tools day to day — to move through a messy dataset faster, draft a first pass at a formula or query, or pressure-test a decision against options I hadn't considered. The judgment is still mine; the tools just cut the time spent on the mechanical parts.

how I work

AI as a working tool, not a headline

Everyone's talking about AI replacing analyst work. What I've found in practice is closer to the opposite — it's how one person can cover ground that used to take a small team, if you still bring the judgment.

In practice that looks like: using an AI assistant to write a first-draft SQL query or Excel formula and then correcting it, having it summarize a messy sales export before I build the dashboard, or running a business decision past it to surface options I hadn't thought of before I commit to one. It doesn't replace checking the numbers myself — it just means I spend that time on the decision instead of on the setup.

projects

Applied to real operations

Inventory findability at scale — Janta Medical Store

2015–2018
situation

The store carried medicines from roughly 500–1,000+ different pharmaceutical companies, each with its own product divisions — close to a million SKUs in total, making it hard to locate a specific medicine quickly.

action

Restructured how inventory records were organized and maintained, improving the system used to track and locate stock across that catalog.

result

Staff could find specific medicines faster within an unusually large, fragmented catalog. From what I recall, better stock organization brought in roughly 10% more daily customers, meaningfully increased revenue, and cut expired stock write-offs by around 90%.

Guest house chain turnaround — Thailand

Managing Director, ST House JMS Group, 2018–2020
situation

Sent by my employer to take over an underperforming guest house chain. The previous owner and staff had been running fake future bookings, and mismanagement and falsified accounts were driving losses.

action

Spent roughly the first 2.5 months getting my work permit and documentation sorted while starting to understand the business and dig into the reports. Once I formally took charge, I traced the reports back to the root cause, introduced quality-control checks on accounts and service delivery, and rebuilt the team — hiring new staff, training myself on the standards I wanted first, then training the team to meet them.

result

Cut out the mismanagement and fake-booking fraud that were driving losses. By the next quarter, occupancy was at around 70% regular check-ins, and the chain went on to profitability, scaling, and new brand partnerships.

Market-driven exit — NT Pharmachem

2020–2025
situation

When COVID hit, tourism shut down and I couldn't return to Thailand, so I stayed in India and started NT Pharmachem — a wholesale pharmaceutical distribution business I built and ran alone for five years. The business saw good growth early on, but two to three years in, margins started getting squeezed by slow payment cycles from hospital clients, on top of broader post-COVID shifts in the medicine market.

action

Analyzed prior years' data and margin trends and concluded that staying in would mean investing more capital into a market where delayed hospital payments and shrinking margins were going to persist, with no clear turnaround in sight.

result

Closed the business on my own terms with the capital recovered rather than lost — avoiding a drawn-out decline instead of reinvesting into it.
